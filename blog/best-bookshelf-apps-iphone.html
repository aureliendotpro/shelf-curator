The best bookshelf apps for iPhone in 2026

A reading tracker, a library catalog, or a shelf you actually want to look at? The right app depends on what you want your books to do for you.

Three kinds of "bookshelf" apps

Search the App Store for "bookshelf" and you'll find dozens of apps that look alike in the results and do very different jobs. Almost all of them fall into one of three families:

  • Reading trackers log what you're reading, how far you've got, and what you thought of it. Goodreads, The StoryGraph, Hardcover and Bookly live here. Their shelves are lists: "Want to read", "Currently reading", "Read".
  • Library catalogs are inventories of the books you own: ISBN, edition, location, who borrowed what. Libib and BookBuddy are the classics. Great for big collections; they look like spreadsheets with covers.
  • Visual shelves show your books as books, on a shelf you arrange and decorate. That's what Shelf Curator does: it's less about logging pages and more about the joy of seeing your collection, your TBR pile and your favourites in one cozy place.

Plenty of readers use two: a tracker for their stats and a shelf for the books they love. There's no wrong answer, only the one that matches your habits.

The apps, one by one

Shelf Curator: for readers who want to see their books

Shelf Curator turns your collection into a bookshelf you curate. You add a book by searching millions of titles, scanning the barcode on the back, or photographing its cover or spine, and it lands on your shelf with its real cover. Rows never run out; each one gets an engraved name plate ("TBR", "Book club", "Summer reads", "Cookbooks"). You can flip a book to show its cover or its spine, make it bigger, add emoji objects, signs and your own photos in frames, and pick from 13 themes and 9 wood finishes, from oak and walnut to LED glow and dark academia.

The detail people notice first: tilt your phone and the books lean, slide and tumble with real physics. Your shelf also lives on your Home Screen as a widget, and you can share it as a ready-to-post picture.

Good to know: there's no account, no ads and no tracking; your shelves stay on your iPhone. It's not a reading tracker: there are no page counts, reading stats or social feed. It's iPhone-only for now, and it needs a subscription after the 7-day free trial.

Goodreads: the big community

Owned by Amazon since 2013, Goodreads is where most readers already have an account, and its strength is scale: ratings and reviews for almost any book, friends' activity, groups, and the yearly Reading Challenge. Your shelves are lists ("Want to read", "Read", plus your own). Many readers find the app dated and ad-heavy, which is why "Goodreads alternative" is such a popular search.

The StoryGraph: for stats lovers

The StoryGraph tracks your reading and turns it into charts: genres, moods, pace, page counts across the year. Its recommendations are based on the mood and pace you're in the mood for, and it imports your Goodreads history in a few taps. The free tier is generous; a Plus subscription adds more stats and features.

Hardcover: a modern tracker

Hardcover is a newer Goodreads-style tracker with a cleaner design, lists, reading goals and a growing community, plus Goodreads import. Free to use, with an optional membership for supporters.

Libib: the catalog for big collections

Libib is built for inventory. Scan barcodes in quick succession, organise books into libraries and tags, and catalog films, music and games too. It works on the web as well as the app, and it's free for personal use. It's practical rather than pretty: think catalog, not cozy corner.

BookBuddy: a detailed home library manager

BookBuddy has been around for years and does the catalog job in depth: barcode scanning, editions, locations, and tracking who borrowed which book. The free version is limited in how many books you can add; Pro is a one-time upgrade.

Bookly: for building a reading habit

Bookly focuses on the act of reading: timed reading sessions, goals, streaks and reports. Useful if you want to read more often; less so if you want a place to show off your collection.

How to choose

  • You want reviews, friends and a yearly challenge: Goodreads, or Hardcover if you want something more modern.
  • You love charts about your reading: The StoryGraph.
  • You own hundreds of books and need an inventory: Libib or BookBuddy.
  • You want to read more often: Bookly.
  • You want your books to feel like a collection again, a shelf you enjoy opening and putting on your Home Screen: Shelf Curator. It pairs well with any tracker above.
