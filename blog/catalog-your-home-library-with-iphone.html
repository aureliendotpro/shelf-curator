How to catalog your home library with your iPhone

Scanning a whole bookcase sounds like a weekend project. With the right method it's closer to an evening, and old books without barcodes don't have to slow you down.

By the Shelf Curator team · Updated November 11, 2026

Before you start

  • Decide what you're cataloging. Everything you own, or just your TBR and favourites? Starting with one bookcase or your TBR pile gets you to the fun part faster.
  • Work shelf by shelf. Take a stack of 10 to 20 books, catalog them, put them back. You always know where you stopped.
  • Good light helps. Barcode and cover recognition work best near a window or under a lamp.

Method 1: scan the ISBN barcode (fastest)

Almost every book printed since the 1970s has an ISBN, and most since the late 1980s carry it as a barcode on the back cover. Point your iPhone camera at it and a good app reads it in under a second and finds the title, author and cover. This is the quickest way through a modern collection.

Tip: if a barcode won't scan (shiny cover, curved spine), type the 13-digit number printed under it instead. It's the same ISBN.

Method 2: photograph the cover or the spine

For books without a barcode (older editions, imports, special editions) take a photo instead. Modern iPhones read text in images on the device, so an app can pick up the title and author from a cover or even a spine and search for the match. Spine photos are great for books packed tightly on a shelf.

Method 3: search by title or author

When all else fails, type it. Open Library and Google Books cover millions of titles in many languages, so even out-of-print books usually turn up. If the cover in the database isn't your edition, use a photo of your own copy as the cover.

Tips for big collections

  • Batch by method: scan all the barcoded books in a stack first, then handle the rest by photo or search.
  • Use shelves for locations or themes ("Living room", "Bedside", "Cookbooks", "Signed copies") so the catalog mirrors your home.
  • Don't chase perfection. Edition details matter for collectors; for most of us, the right cover and title are enough.

Which app should you use?

If you need a detailed inventory (editions, loans, locations, values) a catalog app like Libib or BookBuddy is built for that. If you want your collection to look like a collection, Shelf Curator lets you do all three: scan the barcode, photograph a cover or spine, or search by title. Each book lands on a real-looking shelf with its cover, in rows you name yourself. Your library stays on your iPhone, with no account needed.
