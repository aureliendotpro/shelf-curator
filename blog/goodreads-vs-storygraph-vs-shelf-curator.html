Goodreads vs StoryGraph vs Shelf Curator: an honest comparison

One is a giant social network, one is a stats nerd's dream, and one is a cozy shelf for the books you love. Here's how they really differ.

By the Shelf Curator team · Updated November 11, 2026

Full disclosure: we make Shelf Curator. We use Goodreads and The StoryGraph too, and we've tried to be fair to both. Check the App Store for current prices.

The short answer

  • Goodreads if you want the biggest community: reviews for every book, friends, groups and the Reading Challenge.
  • The StoryGraph if you want to understand your reading: charts, moods, pace, and recommendations that fit them.
  • Shelf Curator if you want to see your books: a real-looking, decorated bookshelf of your collection and TBR, on your phone and your Home Screen.

They're not really rivals. Goodreads and StoryGraph are reading trackers; Shelf Curator is a visual bookshelf. Lots of readers log their reading in one and curate their shelf in the other.

Goodreads: the town square

Goodreads' superpower is that everyone's there. Whatever you're reading, thousands of people have rated it, and your friends' updates show up in your feed. The Reading Challenge is a yearly ritual for millions. The trade-offs are well known: a design that hasn't changed much in years, ads, and Amazon ownership, which some readers prefer to avoid.

The StoryGraph: the reading lab

The StoryGraph took off as the Goodreads alternative for people who love data. Tag books by mood and pace, and it charts your year in reading and recommends what to read next based on how you want to feel. It imports your Goodreads history, so switching is easy. If you finish the year wanting a beautiful breakdown of your reading, this is your app.

Shelf Curator: the cozy corner

Shelf Curator isn't trying to log your pages. It gives your books back what lists take away: the feeling of a shelf. Covers face out, spines stand in a row, each shelf gets an engraved name plate, and a little pot plant holds up the end. Arrange everything by hand, choose a theme (walnut, pastel, dark academia, LED glow and more), and tilt your phone to watch the books lean and tumble.

It's built for the books you own and the ones you can't wait to read: your TBR becomes a shelf you actually want to open. Put it on your Home Screen as a widget, and share it as a picture when someone asks what you're reading next.

Can you use them together?

Yes, and that's how many readers do it: log and rate your reading in Goodreads or The StoryGraph, and keep a curated, beautiful shelf of your collection and TBR in Shelf Curator. One is your reading journal; the other is your bookcase.
